# Why AI answers about brands change

- SparkToro, January 2026 ("New research: AIs are highly inconsistent when recommending brands or products"). In November and December 2025, about 600 volunteers put the same 12 requests for recommendations to ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI, and collected 2,961 answers. ChatGPT or Google's AI gave the same list of brands less than once in 100 tries; the same brands in the same order came back about once in 1,000. Some brands still came up again and again: one hospital was named in 69 of 71 ChatGPT answers about cancer care on the West Coast, but first in only 25. Rand Fishkin of SparkToro ran the study with Patrick O'Donnell of Gumshoe, a company that sells AI tracking. https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/
- Thinking Machines, September 2025 ("Defeating nondeterminism in LLM inference"). One open model was asked about Richard Feynman 1,000 times with temperature at zero and gave 80 different answers; 992 said he was born in Queens, New York, and 8 said New York City. The researchers traced it to how the servers batch requests. https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/
- Google, guide to generative AI features, 10 July 2026: AI Mode runs several related searches for one question (query fan-out), so the pages behind an answer depend on those searches too. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide

What follows: one answer is a sample of one. A fair picture needs many answers, across assistants and over time, and the same questions each time.
