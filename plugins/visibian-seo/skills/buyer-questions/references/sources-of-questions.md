# Where real buyer questions come from

Start with the sources the user owns; they show demand from people who already found the brand. Then look outward.

## The brand's own data

- Google Search Console, Performance report: queries, especially long ones written as questions. Filter queries with words such as how, what, which, best, vs, alternative, cost, price, near. Since 31 August 2026 every site also has a report of impressions in Google's AI answers (Google Search Central blog, 3 June 2026, extended to all sites on 31 August 2026), which shows the pages that appear there, though not the questions.
- Bing Webmaster Tools, AI Performance report (public preview since 10 February 2026): shows a sample of the searches behind citations of the site in Copilot and Bing's AI summaries, and since June 2026 groups them by intent and topic (Bing Webmaster Blog, 10 February and 16 June 2026).
- Site search logs, if the site has a search box.
- Support tickets, live chat and email: the questions people ask before they buy, and the ones that show what the site fails to explain.
- Sales call notes and demo requests: the questions and objections at the decision stage. Remove personal details first.
- Reviews of the brand and its competitors: what buyers say they were looking for, and what they compared.

## Outside sources

- Forums and communities where the buyers talk (Reddit, industry forums, Q&A sites): threads asking for recommendations, with the situation the asker describes.
- "People also ask" and related searches on the search engines.
- Competitors' comparison and FAQ pages: the questions they think buyers ask.
- Trade press and review sites in the category: the criteria they use to compare.

Keep each question with its source, so the list can be checked and refreshed.
