# Where a brand's facts live, in the order to check them

1. The brand's own website: home, about, pricing, contact, legal notice, footer, and the structured data on each. Fully in the brand's hands; fix these first.
2. Search engine profiles: Google Business Profile (for any business with a place customers visit or a service area), Bing Places. Google's guide to generative AI features (10 July 2026) names Business Profile and Merchant Center feeds as ways to help products and services appear in AI responses.
3. Shopping and product feeds: Google Merchant Center for shops; OpenAI asks merchants to send product details for shopping in ChatGPT through a product feed (OpenAI's product feed specification, read 5 November 2026: https://developers.openai.com/commerce/product-feeds/spec).
4. Open knowledge bases: Wikidata (anyone can edit; add facts with references, and follow its notability and conflict-of-interest rules), and Wikipedia only where the brand meets its notability rules and someone without a conflict of interest writes the article.
5. Business directories and profiles: LinkedIn, Crunchbase, industry directories, chambers of commerce, app stores, marketplaces.
6. Review sites for the category: claim the profile, keep facts and categories current, and answer reviews. Never post or buy reviews.
7. Press and partner pages: old press releases, partner and reseller pages, and event listings often carry an old description, an old price or a former executive. Ask their owners to update them.

For each, check: the name as written, the one-sentence description, address and phone, products and prices, the people named, and a link back to the website.
